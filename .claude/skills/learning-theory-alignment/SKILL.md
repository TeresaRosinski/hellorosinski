# Learning-theory alignment

Connect what Teresa actually did to established theory, honestly. Theory explains *why* her choices worked; it must never be stretched to cover something she didn't do.

Use the reference list in `references/theories.md`. Each theory there says what counts as evidence.

## Two modes

### Looking back: a finished session (for the portfolio)
1. List the session's design choices and results (from the draft or the interview).
2. For each theory in the reference list, check its evidence criteria against those choices and results.
3. Rate each match:
   - **Strong:** a specific design choice or result clearly matches the criteria.
   - **Partial:** some criteria are met; say which are missing.
   - **None:** say so; don't force it.
4. Pick the **3–4 strongest** for the "Learning foundations" section. Fewer, well-evidenced theories beat a long list.
5. Write each as: **Theory name:** the specific design choice or result that matches, in one sentence. Name stages or levels precisely (e.g. ADKAR *Desire*, Kirkpatrick *Level 3*).

### Looking ahead: a new design (before she runs it)
1. List the planned activities.
2. Show which theories the plan already uses, and where there are gaps (e.g. "no follow-up planned, so nothing supports ADKAR *Reinforcement* or Kirkpatrick *Level 3*").
3. Suggest one or two small, practical additions per gap. Keep it a short list.

## Rules
- **"Aligned with", not "designed around."** Only say she designed around a theory if she says it was deliberate at the time.
- **Evidence or nothing.** Every claim names a specific design choice, quote, or result.
- **Use each theory in its correct sense.** The reference file lists the common misreadings; avoid them.
- **Partial is honest.** "Strongest at *Desire* and *Knowledge*, with first practice toward *Ability*" is better than claiming all five ADKAR stages.
- **Plain language.** One clause of explanation per theory so a non-L&D reader follows it.

## Output
- A table of all theories considered: theory, rating, evidence (or "none").
- The 3–4 recommended lines for "Learning foundations", ready to paste.
