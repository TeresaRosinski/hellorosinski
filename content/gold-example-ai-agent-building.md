## AI Learning Series · 2023–2026
From 2023 to 2026, I initiated, designed, and led nine AI discussion and learning sessions to help my colleagues at Epsilon learn from each other. It began with one question to the creative technology team: *What do we want to learn together?* AI came up again and again, and my director agreed it should be our focus. After six sessions, I opened the series to the whole creative team.

## AI Agent Building
**From first-timer to your own AI agent in one hour**
*Session 9 · Live virtual workshop · March 2026 · 60 min · 27 participants · Creative team · Microsoft Copilot Agent Builder*

**The situation**
Two weeks earlier, the company had given every employee access to Copilot Agent Builder, and leadership's message was clear: bring AI into everything. But when I talked with my team, I kept hearing the same thing: *we don't know where to start.* So I designed this session. I ran it on my second-to-last day at the company, so I built it to work without me.

**Who was in the room**
27 creatives (art directors, copywriters, front-end developers, producers), from associates to directors, mostly first-timers with agents. And AI replacing people's work was a constant topic, in the news and in conversations at work.

**Learning objectives**
By the end of the session, learners can:
- Generate agent instructions by answering three guiding questions with a ready-made Copilot prompt.
- Build and personalize their own agent in Copilot Agent Builder.
- Share their agent with colleagues.

**Design choices**
- **Read the room, then set the pace.** I opened with two questions: *When you hear "AI agents," what thoughts come to mind?* and *Have you experimented with creating AI agents?* To the second, 19 people answered: 3 yes, 14 no, 2 maybe. I usually open the floor every few slides and wait for three people to share before moving on, so the group learns from each other. With mostly first-timers, there was less experience to draw on, so I waited for one or two and kept the build moving.
- **Reframe "agent" as "assistant."** In the opening word cloud, the most common answers were "assistant" and "helper," next to "suspicious," "insecurity," "confusion," and "Agent Smith from The Matrix." The next question built on the helpful side: *If you could have an assistant help you with anything, what would it be?* Answers appeared live on screen, then people unmuted to share.
- **Let the group choose.** In a 60-second live poll, participants chose between three agents: a *Skill Sidekick* to help you improve something you want to learn, a *Deadline Therapist* for those "how is this due tomorrow?" moments, or a *Creative Partner* for ideas, writing, or design inspiration. The Skill Sidekick won. We built it together, and each person made it their own.
- **Skip the lecture, start building.** No explanation of what agents are; we went straight to building one together.
- **Remove the biggest barrier.** From my own experience and conversations with colleagues, I knew where people struggled with AI: explaining clearly what they need. Writing clear instructions takes time, and it's hard to know what to focus on or how to structure them. So I took that off their plate. Each participant:
  1. Answered three simple questions in a shared template, with example answers to keep things moving:
     - *When would this agent be helpful?*
     - *What kind of helper do I want?*
     - *How should it respond when helping you?*
  2. Gave those answers to Copilot with a ready-made prompt, and Copilot wrote the agent's instructions for them.
  3. Pasted the instructions into Agent Builder, added extra context, and learned how to share the agent with colleagues.

**Results**
- We completed every step together within the hour, so everyone who followed along left with a working agent and knew how to share it. *"That was amazing. In one hour I built my own agent start to finish."* (participant)
- *"You hit this out of the park."* (a director, on open mic)
- **Afterwards:** a request for a follow-up check-in, a one-on-one brainstorm for a live project, and requests from several people (some through my manager) for the recording and resources.
- **More than six months after I left the company, a former colleague told me her team was building an agent. When someone started overcomplicating it, a teammate said: *"Why don't we just use what Teresa showed us?"***

**Learning foundations**
- **Adult learning principles (Knowles):** the opening question started from participants' own work, not from the tool.
- **Self-determination theory:** *autonomy* (the group vote, then personal choices), *competence* (a working agent in one hour), *connection* (sharing ideas live and sharing agents with colleagues).
- **Worked examples (cognitive load theory):** the template and example answers removed the blank-page problem, so attention went to building.
- **ADKAR:** strongest at *Desire* and *Knowledge*, with first practice toward *Ability*. *Reinforcement* would come from follow-up, which participants asked for.

**What I'd do next**
- **Follow up with a quick poll:** how many people built a working agent, what would make a follow-up session valuable, and what questions or confusion remain. It would give me a real completion number and start the reinforcement participants asked for.
- **For returning learners:** small groups that choose their own use cases and refine instructions together, moving from guided steps to independence.
